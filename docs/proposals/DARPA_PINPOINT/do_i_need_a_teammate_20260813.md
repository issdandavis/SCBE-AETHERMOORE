# Do I need a teammate?

Asked 2026-08-13. Facts: CAGE `1EXD5`, UEI `J4NXHM6N5F59`, prior DARPA abstracts, academic email, incoming Peninsula College freshman, maybe college grant help.

## Short answer

**No for eligibility. Yes if the goal is a fundable month-12 six-axis lab demo while you are a full-time freshman.**

You can file alone. The BAA says all responsible sources may propose, including small businesses. Being a community-college freshman does not bar you. CAGE + UEI + a conforming abstract is enough to get in the door.

DARPA will then score, in order: technical merit, mission relevance, **cost and schedule realism**, **capabilities / related experience**. Those last two are where a teammate buys something your CAGE cannot.

## What a teammate would actually buy

Someone who already has, or can rent without drama:

- a bench that can hold a magnetic trap
- a rate table or a real path to one
- a person who has shipped a sensor, not only a paper

That is the month-12 problem. It is not a status problem.

A Peninsula instructor as **lab-access / letter** is useful. A Peninsula instructor listed as co-PI with no IMU bench is decoration. Do not put the college on the cover sheet unless a named person owns a real work package.

Collin / DAVA is firmware. Wrong plant. Do not reopen that for PINPOINT.

## Freshman year is a schedule fact

If this awards, Phase I is 24 months of 1.0 FTE device work across your first two college years. Classes are not a disqualifier. They are a realism item. The abstract should say: sole PI, full-time student, bench in Port Angeles, test days rented, APEX for contracting paperwork. Hiding the courseload is worse than naming it.

College "research grant help" is usually a faculty NSF / Space Grant / student-research award. That can buy coils and fluid. It cannot replace the DARPA month-12 demo. Treat it as materials money, not as a second PI.
